Idle Inventory.

Birkenstock has not adjusted to shifting consumer behavior around secondhand, has not de-risked its supply chain, and has costly inventory sitting with no productive exit channel.

Consumers are already buying your products secondhand at scale. The demand is proven. The only question is whether Birkenstock captures it — or continues to let third parties capture it instead. Meanwhile, DTC growth has slowed and margins are tightening. Resale is a ready-made lever that requires no new production.

Liquidation recovers pennies on the dollar and actively undermines brand positioning. It puts Birkenstock product in discount channels without brand control, damages perceived premium value, and recovers a fraction of the capital already invested in production. Branded resale recovers significantly more revenue while keeping the customer relationship and brand experience in-house.

Evidence across enterprise footwear brands shows the opposite. Branded resale attracts a different consumer — one who discovered the brand through a lower price point and becomes a mainline customer. Resale customers who also shop mainline show 2.8× higher lifetime value than mainline-only customers. It expands the funnel, it doesn't shrink it.

02 — Recommended Approach

A Phased Path
to Scale

All enterprise footwear brands that have launched resale started the same way: activating inventory already on hand. Birkenstock is ideally positioned to do this faster than most.

01
Launch Phase
Brand-Owned Inventory Resale
Leverage returns, damages, and excess inventory to seed the resale channel. Demand modeling shows the market can absorb 70,000+ units in year 1. Two logistics models available — choose based on current warehouse capabilities and desired margin profile.
Option A — In-House
Integrate Archive's WMS into your existing warehouse. Lower per-unit cost, more operational control. Best if current processing capabilities are strong.
Option B — 3PL Partner
Leverage a vetted 3PL specialist in reverse logistics via Archive's WMS. Higher per-unit cost, but scales easily and handles cleaning/repair workflows.
02
Scale Phase
Add Trade-In (In-Store & Mail-In)
Adds a powerful second supply source and creates a new brand touchpoint. Customers trade in worn pairs and receive a gift card — then on average spend 2.5–3× the gift card value on mainline. Drives store traffic, same-day purchases, and measurable loyalty lift. Trade-in becomes an additive revenue driver to the overall business, not just a resale mechanic.

04 — Strategic Benefits

Beyond the
Revenue Line

Resale isn't just a margin play. Across every enterprise footwear brand that has launched, the downstream effects on customer acquisition, loyalty, and mainline revenue are equally compelling.

>50%
Net-New Customer Acquisition
On average, more than half of branded resale customers are new to the brand. One comparable global footwear brand acquires net-new customers through resale at a higher rate than through its mainline channel — at a fraction of the CAC.
2.8×
Higher Lifetime Value
Customers who shop both a brand's resale channel and mainline have 2.8× higher LTV than mainline-only customers. Resale creates a stickier, more engaged consumer relationship — not a cheaper one.
2.5–3×
Trade-In Upspend on Mainline
Every €1 of brand credit given to customers who trade in drives €2.5–3 in mainline revenue. Trade-in creates a virtuous loop: customers offload old pairs, receive credit, and immediately invest back in the brand.
2+ yrs
Lapsed Customer Reactivation
Resale has a unique ability to re-engage customers who have been inactive for 2 or more years. New ways to participate with the brand — at a different price point — bring lapsed shoppers back into the active customer base.
